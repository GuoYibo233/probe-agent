Below is my interpretation

The final code's speed and alignment results match the smoke tests, and none of the six comparison numbers regressed. The probe fix is effective (raising the two budgets by 3.3 and 1.4 GB respectively) but did not catch up to the training peak: the probe picks by "the block with the most tokens," while the block with the largest memory footprint might be the one with more rows and more loss positions (the loss positions' logits grow with the target token count) -- this is a guess, not measured. Decision 25: the probe is not changed again this round; it is defined as a lower-bound estimate with a known practical bias, and card assignment adds 10% to the probe's fullest-block number before judging headroom (assistant 1 computed: the two budgets' biases are 7.5% and 5.8%, 54.38 × 1.1 = 59.81, higher than 58.47, and 76.47 × 1.1 = 84.12, higher than 80.91; this 10% only covers the gap between allocated peaks, the discount for reserved fragmentation is a separate layer, written as two separate rules in stage-commands); the fallback "run two real minibatches through the actual training loop" is listed among the things that need gyb's ruling.
